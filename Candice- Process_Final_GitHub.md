## Purpose

Completing an electrical engineering degree does not automatically prepare you for every qualification expected in distribution engineering. Employers may seek technical skills, practical experience, and professional preparation beyond coursework. Without a focused plan, you risk overlooking these requirements before graduation. This guide presents three best practices to help you build relevant technical knowledge, gain practical experience, and prepare for entry-level distribution engineer employment.

## Build Technical Knowledge

### Electrical Engineering Fundamentals

Review entry-level distribution engineering job postings to identify essential subjects, such as circuits and power systems. 

Prioritize B EE 477 (Power System Fundamentals) and B EE 478 (Power System Analysis) after completing foundational courses such as B EE 233 (Circuit Theory) [1]. These courses develop knowledge of three-phase systems, power flow, and fault analysis, which is valuable for distribution engineering. Review course prerequisites and plan your electives accordingly.

### Safety Codes and Design Tools

Knowledge of safety standards and design software helps engineers prepare accurate designs that meet utility requirements.

Review the National Electrical Safety Code (NESC) [2] to understand distribution safety requirements. Identify commonly requested design tools, such as AutoCAD or ArcGIS, from job postings. Select one tool, complete an introductory tutorial, and save your work to document progress.

### Engineering Calculations

Accurate voltage-drop and fault-current calculations support reliable distribution system design and help engineers identify potential problems.

Practice these calculations using course materials and compare your answers with verified solutions. If errors occur, review the relevant concepts and repeat the calculations.

## Gain Practical Experience

### Utility Internships

Utility internships provide opportunities to apply classroom knowledge to engineering projects and gain experience valued by employers.

Search for electrical engineering internships on official utility websites, including Snohomish County PUD and Seattle City Light [3], [4]. Check application deadlines and required qualifications regularly. Tailor your resume to highlight relevant coursework and engineering projects and submit applications before the deadlines.

### Learning Through Internships

Documenting technical challenges and receiving feedback helps you identify weaknesses and improve your engineering skills.

Keep a weekly record of assigned tasks, challenges, and lessons learned. Request supervisor feedback and identify areas for improvement. Participate in field activities only when authorized and supervised.

## Prepare for Professional Employment

### FE Exam Preparation

Taking the FE exam around graduation helps you retain and apply recently learned engineering fundamentals. Griffin Zheng, a senior electrical engineer at Seattle City Light, recommends this timing while the material remains fresh [7].

Review the FE Electrical and Computer exam specifications on the NCEES website [5]. Identify weak topics, use Electrical FE Review [6] and NCEES practice questions, and adjust your study schedule based on incorrect answers.

### Career Readiness

Aligning your resume with employer requirements helps demonstrate how your engineering skills and project experience match the qualifications for distribution engineering positions.

Review job postings and tailor your resume to highlight relevant coursework, engineering projects, and measurable results. Keep records of project outcomes to support your applications. If a required skill is missing, identify a relevant course or project to address the gap.

## Conclusion

A successful transition from college to distribution engineering requires more than completing a degree. By following a focused learning plan, you develop the technical skills, practical experience, and professional qualifications needed to compete for entry-level positions. Regularly reviewing your progress and adjusting your priorities helps you stay prepared as industry expectations change.
